# Behavioral and Economic Analysis of Employee Attrition

## Overview
This repository contains an independent behavioral consulting case study on employee attrition.

I analyzed a synthetic IBM HR dataset to identify employee groups associated with higher attrition, interpret those patterns using behavioral and economic concepts, and propose practical retention interventions that an organization could test.

## Business question
Which employee characteristics and workplace experiences are associated with employee attrition, and which behavioral intervention should an organization test first to improve retention?

## Analysis
I calculated and compared attrition rates across:

- Overtime status
- Job satisfaction
- Work-life balance
- Years at company
- Commute distance from home
- Monthly income group

The analysis uses descriptive comparisons. It identifies associations and patterns; it does not prove that any factor caused employees to leave.

## Behavioral and economic lens
The findings were interpreted using:

- **Friction and effort cost:** Overtime, long commutes, and poor work-life balance can make staying in a job more demanding.
- **Motivation and perceived value:** Job satisfaction can indicate whether employees experience their work as rewarding or worthwhile.
- **Belonging and early commitment:** Employees early in their tenure may need clearer support, expectations, and social connection.
- **Perceived fairness:** Income and rewards may affect whether employees feel the benefits of staying match the effort required.

## Recommendations
1. **Reduce workload friction:** Pilot workload reviews, protected no-meeting blocks, and a clearer process for escalating unsustainable workload.
2. **Strengthen early-tenure support:** Pilot 30/60/90-day manager check-ins and a peer-buddy system for new employees.
3. **Increase perceived future value:** Introduce structured career-development conversations and clearer internal-mobility opportunities.

## Limitations
- The dataset is synthetic.
- The analysis is observational and descriptive.
- The findings show associations, not causation.
- The dataset does not include exit interviews, manager quality, personal circumstances, organizational culture, or labor-market conditions.
- Recommendations should be tested through employee research and a controlled pilot before broader implementation.
